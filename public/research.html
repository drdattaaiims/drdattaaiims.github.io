Research

I build and evaluate AI systems for radiology, and study where they fail once they leave a benchmark and meet real clinical data. Most of the work below sits at the boundary between a working model and a system a clinician can actually trust: labelled data is scarce, deployment conditions shift, and language-model outputs need governance before they reach a report. I do this as Lab Lead of CRASH Lab at Ashoka University, and as a Visiting Researcher with the Rajpurkar Lab at Harvard Medical School.

Fine-grained diagnosis under label scarcity

Rib fractures are undercounted on routine CT because fine-grained, per-fracture annotation is expensive and few centres produce it at scale. AIRib, developed with IIT Jodhpur and AIIMS Delhi under a ₹3.9M TIH iHub Drishti grant, is an end-to-end pipeline for detecting, characterising, and prognosticating traumatic rib fractures from CT with substantially less labelled data than standard training requires, using hyperbolic embeddings to encode the fracture hierarchy directly into the model.

→ MICCAI 2025 (accepted, top 9% of submissions); RSNA Trainee Research Prize 2023 — the only award to an Indian resident that year. Publications →

Agentic workflows and their failure modes in radiology reporting

Large language models are increasingly used inside radiology reporting pipelines — for speech-to-text cleanup, structured-report generation, and decision support. I work on where these agentic pipelines break under distribution shift, and what governance their failure modes imply before they're allowed near a clinical report.

→ RSNA 2023, two Cutting-Edge Oral presentations on LLM-augmented speech recognition for radiology reporting; "From Chatbots to Agentic Workflows: Ensuring Responsible Deployment of Large Language Models in Radiology," Indian Journal of Radiology & Imaging, 2025. Publications →

Multimodal foundation models for medical image interpretation

With the Rajpurkar Lab at Harvard Medical School, I work on generalist foundation models that interpret medical images across modalities rather than one narrow task at a time — including MedVersa, developed with Hong-Yu Zhou, Julian Acosta, Subathra Adithan, Eric Topol, and Pranav Rajpurkar.

→ MedVersa: A Generalist Foundation Model for Medical Image Interpretation (under review, NEJM AI). Publications →

LLM reliability in radiology practice and education

Before a language model is trusted with a clinical decision, its reliability needs to be measured against the specific task, not a general benchmark. I've evaluated LLM accuracy on FRCR examination questions and on imaging-referral decisions for pulmonary embolism, both with Sarangi PK and colleagues.

→ "Evaluating ChatGPT-4's Performance in Identifying Radiological Anatomy in FRCR Part 1 Examination Questions" and "Radiologic Decision-Making for Imaging in Pulmonary Embolism," Indian Journal of Radiology & Imaging, 2024–2025. Publications →
